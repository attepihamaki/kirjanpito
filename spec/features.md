# App features: overview, year handling, imports, archive, checks

Part of the project specification. `CLAUDE.md` is the index and holds the conventions; section numbers are the same in every file, and code comments cite them as §N.

### 8.12 Overview (Yleiskatsaus)

The app's front page, at `/app/overview`. `/app`, "Jatka kirjanpitoa", opening a file and starting a new bookkeeping all land here. It answers three questions: how the year is going, what VAT is due and when, and what is left to do. Every widget is a summary that links into a view; nothing here edits a figure except the filed mark.

- **`components/overview/`.** `overview-data.tsx` computes everything once, in a context, from the selected year: the summary, the income statement, VAT per month, and the VAT timeline from today (§6.5). The widgets read it.
- **Widgets**, in their default order and size:
  - **Tulos**: line 358 or 359 from 1.1. to today, or to 31.12. for a past year; the formula line; a sparkline of the cumulative result; the entry count, the profit margin and the year's VAT. With entries in the year before, also its result to the same date and its same month, beside this year's (`sameTimeLastYear` in `lib/domain/year-compare.ts`), so part of a year is never set against all of the last. Links to Tuloslaskelma.
  - **Verot ja YEL** (`tax-widget.tsx`, hidden by default; shown from "Muokkaa näkymää"): the income tax the profit so far adds, on the year's scale and the user's details (§6.14), with its share of the profit; the whole year at the same pace; the advance tax booked (line 365) and what is still to set aside, or that the advances cover it; the YEL contribution a month; a breakdown under "Erittely". Without a municipal rate it asks for one and links to Asetukset → Kalenteri; a year with no scale says so.
  - **Seuraava ALV-ilmoitus**: the next period not yet filed, its amount as payable, refundable or "Nollailmoitus", a due-date row with the weekday and a days-left pill (amber at 14 days or fewer, red with "pv myöhässä" once overdue), a note when EU or reverse-charge sales must be reported too, and the period after it. "Merkitse ilmoitetuksi" for a closed period, with "Peru" right after. Links to ALV-laskelma on that period.
  - **Pankkitili**: the bank balance the user typed in, with its date (`bankBalance`, `lib/domain/bank-balance.ts`). The app has no connection to a bank, and a static export could not hold one. With entries paid after the reading and up to today, the big figure is an estimate for today, labelled as one, with the balance and the entries' gross total under it: an open entry is left out until it is paid, and tax payments other than booked VAT (§6.6) are not in the book. The owner's withdrawals and investments count once booked (§6.7), and a payment made in cash does not count (§8.5). Lines where no money moves (312 to 315, 337, 353, 354) stay out; fixed assets count. The balance goes in `settings.json`, not on any report.
  - **Kuukaudet** (wide, two rows tall): `IncomeExpenseChart`, shared with Yhteenveto. Net income above the axis and net expenses below it, one axis. **The bars are mirrored on purpose**: the income green and expense red fail a deuteranopia check side by side (ΔE 5.3 light, 3.5 dark, against 8), and position gives the two series a difference every reader sees. Expenses are also hatched and income solid, with the series names in the plot and the largest bar of each side labelled with its amount, so no cue is colour alone. The legend swatches match. A hover gives the month's income, expenses, VAT and result, the result by the income statement's rules (`monthFigures` in `summary.ts`), so the months add up to 358; a click opens the ledger on that month. Four figures under it: the best month, average income and expenses per elapsed month, months with entries.
  - **Myynti**: line 300 split into Suomi, EU, muut maat and käänteinen, as a stacked bar and a list; lines 301 to 324 below as other income.
  - **Kulut ryhmittäin**: the §6.4 buckets with expenses, largest first; empty ones are counted.
  - **Viimeisimmät tositteet** (wide): the year's six newest entries, signed gross, flagged "ei liitettä" where there is no receipt file. A row opens the entry dialog.
  - **Tarkistettavaa**: unsaved changes with a save (or download) button; each overdue return and each return due within 30 days, with "Ilmoitettu"; one row per check of §8.4 that flags anything (no receipt, a rate that does not fit the date, a future date, a domestic sale without VAT, an unusually large amount, a possible duplicate, VAT on a cost whose VAT cannot be deducted), each with "Näytä", which opens the ledger filtered to them; receipts missing from the file; the VAT registration limit (below); empty months from the first entry to the last complete month, skipping any before the start month; and one row per closed year with entries, as a reminder outside the open count (`yearsToArchive` in `lib/domain/retention.ts`). Every row that is not an entry check or a limit is a rule of `OVERVIEW_RULES` (§6.10). It gives the dates of KPL 2:10 (vero.fi, "Kirjanpito, tilikausi, verokausi"), the books until 31.12. ten years on and the vouchers six, and asks for a copy of the file that is no longer edited. "Säilytyspaketti" downloads the year's retention package (§8.21). "Tallessa" marks the year in `yearsArchived` and the toast can undo it. A year past the 10 years is not listed. The journal and the general ledger print the same dates (§8.4).
- **The limits of KPL 1:1a** (`lib/domain/book-limits.ts`). Each counts only when it holds in both the closed year and the one before. Single-entry books are allowed unless two of these are exceeded: assets 100 000 €, turnover 200 000 €, more than 3 staff. Financial statements are required when two of the micro limits are: 450 000 €, 900 000 €, 10 staff. They then follow the micro rules, within 4 months. The book does not record staff, so two limits over gets an error row saying the app does not cover it, and one over gets an information row naming the staff limit. Assets are Form 5's 718, which has no bank deposits, so a business near a limit has to count those in itself.
- **The filing period's limit** (`vatPeriodOverLimit`). Quarterly filing needs turnover of at most 100 000 € and yearly filing at most 30 000 € (vero.fi, "Tax period"). A closed year above that gets a warning row, and nothing is blocked, since the period itself is set in OmaVero.
- **Form 5's due date** (`lib/domain/tax-return-deadline.ts`). A row from the start of the year until Verohallinto's date, a row per tax year in `data/rules.json`. A year not in the table says "in the spring" until the end of April rather than guessing.
- **The VAT registration limit** (`lib/domain/vat-threshold.ts`). A business is liable for VAT from the day a calendar year's turnover exceeds 20 000 €, and while the previous year's did (AVL 3 §, vero.fi, "VAT exemption for small businesses", valid from 1.1.2025). The book does not record registration, so a line 300 sale with VAT this year or last is what says the business is registered, and the row then disappears. Otherwise the checklist has one row, following today rather than the selected year: turnover against the limit as information, a warning from 15 000 €, and from the sale that goes over, the date it did, as an error. Turnover is line 300 net, without `300-eu-services`, which is supplied in the buyer's country. The book cannot tell a service sold outside the EU from an exported good, or a fixed asset from other sales, so those count.
- **The board** (`overview-board.tsx`) is a three-column grid with `grid-flow-dense` from 1100 px, one column below it. "S" spans one column and "L" two; the month chart at "L" also spans two rows, so the default fills the grid with no holes. Widgets use container queries for their own narrow layouts, so a widget adapts to its column, not the window.
- **Editing the layout.** "Muokkaa näkymää" turns on edit mode. Each widget gets a toolbar: a drag handle, arrows to move it among the visible widgets, widen or narrow (hidden below 1100 px, where size means nothing), and hide. Its contents go `inert`, so a drag never opens an entry. Hidden widgets wait as buttons under the board. "Palauta oletus" resets; "Valmis" leaves. The layout is `overviewLayout`, saved in `settings.json` and dirty like any setting. dnd-kit's screen-reader texts are translated (`overview.dnd*`).
- **Empty book**: no widgets and no edit button, but the two ways in and three short steps.
- `test/overview.test.tsx` covers the §10 result, the countdown, filing and undoing, the checklist, hiding and moving widgets, and the empty state.

### 8.14 Opening a year (Vuoden aloitus)

`components/new-year/new-year-dialog.tsx` (`DialogState` kind `new-year`, with the year to open) and `lib/domain/new-year.ts`, pure and tested. **The year to open is not the selected one**: it is the current year until 31 October and the next from 1 November (`newYearTarget`), so on 4.11.2026 it is 2027. Asetukset, Yleiset has a panel "Uusi vuosi" with the button at any time. The overview's checklist has an information row from November to February while the year before has entries and the year has no year-end figures (`newYearDue`).

Most of a new year's starting point carries over without any action, and the first step only shows it: the asset pool's opening balance (§8.8), the loans outstanding (§6.8), the open receivables and payables on 31.12. (they count in the year they are paid, §6.3), the year before's result, and whether its Form 5 is marked as filed. Templates have no end date (§8.5) and voucher numbers restart at 1 (§4).

- **Figures.** What does not carry is the year-end figures typed in on Varat ja velat (§8.8). The second step lists last year's and this year's side by side, and "Kopioi" copies last year's as this year's starting values through `setYearEnd`, so the book becomes dirty. It asks first when the year already has figures, and it is disabled for a year whose Form 5 is marked as filed (§8.11) or when last year has none.
- **Recurring.** The third step lists the templates with an editable amount (`RecurringAmountInput`, shared with Asetukset, Toistuvat) and opens the recurring dialog for the current month of the year, or January, to book what is due (§8.5).
- Nothing new is stored in `settings.json`, and the stock change is not booked here.
- `test/new-year.test.tsx` covers the dialog and `lib/domain/new-year.test.ts` the figures.

### 8.15 Getting started (Aloita tästä)

`components/overview/onboarding-checklist.tsx` and `lib/domain/onboarding.ts`, pure and tested. A panel above the overview's widgets, shown after "Aloita uusi kirjanpito" and whether or not the setup dialog was skipped. There is no sample ledger (§10), so the checklist teaches with examples written as text.

- **Five steps**, each done by what is in the book, never ticked by hand: business details set, a first sale, a first cost, a receipt attached and available, and a saved file (a file name and no unsaved changes). Sales and costs on lines that sit off the statement (§5) do not count.
- **Each step has an example of what to type** (Lasku 1001 – Mäkinen Rakennus Oy, 2 510,00 € with 25,5 %, and so on) and a button. The buttons open the entry dialog with the type, category and VAT rate filled in, or Asetukset, or save. The description and the amount stay empty, so a click cannot book an invoice that did not happen.
- **`onboarding` in the store is ephemeral.** `newBook` sets it, "Piilota lista" clears it, and opening a file or closing the book clears it too, so a file brought from elsewhere is never nagged. A reload drops it. When every step is done the panel says so and stays until closed.
- `lib/domain/onboarding.test.ts` covers the steps and `test/setup-dialog.test.tsx` the flag.

### 8.16 Bank statement import (Tuo tiliote)

`lib/domain/bank-csv.ts`, `lib/domain/bank-match.ts`, `lib/commands/bank-commands.ts` and `components/bank/`. "Tuo tiliote" sits in the ledger's header beside "Uusi tosite" and opens `DialogState` kind `bank-import` with the file's text. The first user is the one sole trader this app is for, so the aim is typing less: a statement row either marks an open entry paid or becomes a new entry.

- **Nothing is booked unattended.** The dialog is a review list with one row per transaction. Every row has an action (skip, new voucher, or paid for one of the open entries that fit), and "Kirjaa" books what the list says. One toast undoes the whole booking, new entries and payment dates together.
- **A statement opened like a bookkeeping file is recognised.** `openFile` reads a CSV that the ledger importer calls `not-a-book` and, when `parseBankCsv` accepts it, opens the review list on the ledger instead of refusing it, before the unsaved-changes prompt since nothing is replaced. With no bookkeeping open there is nothing to match against, so a dialog says to open or start one first (`claimBankStatement`).
- **Parsing is lenient about rows and strict about the shape**, like the ledger CSV (§7.1). `parseBankCsv` takes the text from `decodeCsv`, detects the delimiter, and skips and counts a row with no usable date or amount. Amounts are signed, with money in positive.
- **OP's export is a preset**, recognised by its header (`Kirjauspäivä`, `Määrä EUROA`, `Saaja/Maksaja`, `Arkistointitunnus` and the rest): `;` delimited, UTF-8 with a BOM, ISO dates, a decimal comma, and `ref=` where the reference is empty. Any other layout gets a mapping step: a guess from common Finnish and English header names, which the user corrects. A mapping needs a date and either one amount column or a debit and a credit column. Only OP's layout has been tested against a real file. Nordea, S-Pankki and Danske have no preset.
- **An ISO 20022 statement, camt.053, is read as well** (`lib/domain/bank-camt.ts`, `parseBankStatement`). A file whose root is a `Document` in a `camt.053` namespace goes to `parseCamt053` instead of the CSV reader, whatever its name; "Tuo tiliote", the file pickers and a drop take `.xml`. The domain layer has no DOM, so the module carries a small XML reader of its own (elements, attributes, text, CDATA, comments and the predefined entities; a DOCTYPE's internal subset is never read). Every entry (`Ntry`) of every statement (`Stmt`) is one row: the booking date, the value date, the amount signed by `CdtDbtInd` and turned round by `RvslInd`, the debtor on money in or the creditor on money out as the counterparty (with or without `Pty`), the structured creditor reference, the unstructured message lines joined, the entry's `AcctSvcrRef` as the bank's id, and `AddtlNtryInf` as the kind. An entry carrying several transactions whose own amounts add up to it, such as a day's reference payments credited as one, becomes one row each. A pending entry (`Sts` not `BOOK`) and one not in euros are skipped and counted. The statement's IBAN, its opening balance (`OPBD`, or `PRCD`) and its closing balance (`CLBD`) come along as `StatementInfo`, the first statement's opening and the last one's closing when one file holds several statements of one account. No bank's real file has been tried: the reader was written from the schema and a synthetic statement (`bank-camt.test.ts`).
- **Reconciliation** (`lib/domain/bank-reconcile.ts`, `components/bank/bank-reconcile.tsx`). Under the review list, closed to start with, "Täsmäytys" sets the statement against the book over its days: from the opening balance's date, or the first row's booking or value date, to the closing balance's date, or the last row's. The book's side is every entry paid on those days through the bank (`movesBank`: not cash, not a non-cash line), the parts of a split receipt paid on one day as one payment, plus the rows the review is about to book, so the figures follow the actions as the user picks them. Every other row is paired with a payment of the same signed amount paid on its booking or value date, each payment used once. It shows the opening balance, the rows' sum and the closing balance, the book's movement and the difference; says when the rows do not take the opening balance to the closing one (rows missing from the file, or skipped); counts the rows neither in the book nor chosen for booking; and lists the payments in the book that the statement has no row for, for the user to check the date, cash or another account. The heading says whether the two agree or by how much they differ. With a closing balance, "Käytä loppusaldoa pankkitilin saldona" sets the overview's bank balance (§8.12) to it. Nothing is stored.
- **Matching** (`proposeForRows`): an open entry on the same side with the same gross amount, on a line that can be unpaid (§6.3). The text decides between several: a number of four digits or more in the bank's message or reference that appears in the entry's description, or a description that names the counterparty. One agreeing entry is "likely", a single entry that only the amount fits is "amount", and anything else is ambiguous and starts as skip. An open entry is claimed by one row at most.
- **An invoice is matched by its reference** (§8.27). Before the rules above, a row of money in whose reference field, or whose message, carries an issued invoice's reference number, and whose amount equals what the invoice still owes (the gross of its unpaid entries), is proposed as paid with the status "reference". The row's action is the invoice's first open entry. "Kirjaa" (`withInvoiceEntries`) adds the invoice's other open entries, all paid on the day the money arrived, so a two-rate invoice closes whole and one toast undoes all of it. A part payment or an overpayment is not matched this way and goes through the ordinary rules. A statement read again sees the paid invoice as booked.
- **Already booked** rows start as skip. A row counts as booked when an entry on the same side and gross amount was paid on the row's date. It covers statements that were never booked from here, such as entries typed by hand; the bank's transaction id is kept in `bankSeen` below and not on the entry, which would change the CSV.
- **A row reviewed before is recognised.** `bankSeen` in `settings.json` (§7.6) holds a fingerprint for every row of each statement the user booked from (`rowFingerprints`): `id:` and the bank's own transaction id when the file has one, otherwise `fp:` and the date, amount, counterparty, reference and message, with the row's order among identical rows so two equal payments in one statement stay apart. A row whose fingerprint is stored gets the status "seen" and starts as skip, whatever else fits, so an overlapping or repeated statement shows only its new rows; the user may still change its action. The rows are recorded with the booking, skipped ones included, and the undo toast forgets them again. Nothing is stored on the entries, so the CSV is unchanged. A booking that books nothing records nothing.
- **A counterparty in the register comes before history** (`registerParty`, §8.29): a party on the row's side whose name the counterparty contains, the longest name winning, gives its category and rate for that side (`partyDefaults`) when the user's rules give none, and the booked voucher links to it (`Entry.party`). A party with no category for the side falls through to history.
- **Suggestions come from the book's own history** (`suggestFromHistory`): earlier entries on the same side whose description names the counterparty give the category (the commonest) and the newest entry's rate. Nothing is stored. A counterparty with no rule and no history gets a guess from its words (`suggestFromWords`, the same hints as `suggestCategory`, §8.5): expenses only, enabled categories only, never an EU purchase. The row says the category was guessed, and the rate is the category's own on that date. A counterparty the hints do not know has no suggestion, and the row cannot be booked until a category is picked or the row is skipped. A new entry is gross, dated on the booking date, and described as "counterparty – message". With no history the category's rate on that date is used (§5).
- **A VAT payment or refund is recognised** (`unsettledDues`, §6.6). A row of money out that equals a closed period's tax, or money in that equals its refund, arriving after that period ended, is proposed as a new voucher on the settlement category with the period set. Each period is used once, oldest first. The booked voucher carries the period's VAT return as a PDF, as the return dialog's button does. Nothing is matched by text, so a part payment is not recognised.
- **Categories offered** are the enabled ones on the row's side, except EU purchases, whose amount is net and whose tax the buyer reports (§6.9). Pick those in the entry dialog.
- **Locks** (§8.11). A payment date is allowed in a filed VAT period, since it reports nothing new, but not in or out of a year whose Form 5 is filed (`paidChangeHitsForm5`). A new entry in a stored or Form 5 filed year refuses the whole booking with a toast. A change that adds to a filed return asks first.
- **Not handled:** several bank accounts (the IBAN is read and not used), camt.052 and camt.054, attaching the statement as the voucher of a VAT payment, an owner's transfer or a loan row, splitting one row across entries, recognising the owner's own name as a transfer, and the other banks' presets.

### 8.17 Category rules (Säännöt)

`lib/domain/category-rules.ts`, pure and tested, and `components/settings/rule-settings.tsx`. A rule says that text appearing in a bank row or an invoice belongs to a category: "a description containing Elisa goes to 344-other at 25,5 %". The user makes them on Asetukset, Säännöt; nothing creates one by itself.

- **A rule** is `contains`, `catId` and `vatRate`, where a null rate leaves the category's own default (dated, §5) in force. They are `categoryRules` in `settings.json` (§7.6), dirty like any setting. Reading drops a rule with no usable text, no category id or a rate that is not 0 to 100.
- **Matching** folds case, accents and punctuation (`foldText`) and looks for the text anywhere in the other. Text under two characters never matches. The side comes from the category, so a rule has no type of its own.
- **The first rule in the user's order wins** (`findCategoryRule`), and the settings page moves a rule up or down. A rule whose category is missing, switched off or on the wrong side for the row is skipped and a later one may fit. The settings table marks such a rule.
- **The bank import** (§8.16) reads the counterparty and the message and tries the rules before `suggestFromHistory`. The review list still lets the user change every proposal.
- **The PDF pre-fill** (§8.5) reads the seller and the invoice rows and tries the rules before the seller's earlier entry and before `suggestCategory`. A rule's own rate beats the rate read off the invoice, except on an EU purchase category, which keeps the category's rate (§6.9).
- **Rules never touch booked vouchers.** They only fill what a new entry starts with.
- `lib/domain/category-rules.test.ts` covers the matching, the order, the bank proposals and the settings round trip.

### 8.18 Click-through on figures (Tositteet luvun takana)

KPL 2:6 has the link between transactions, vouchers and entries, through any sub-ledgers to the general ledger and on to the financial statements, easy to establish in both directions, and says the same of a periodic return made from the books to an authority. A figure on the VAT view, the VAT return, the income statement and Form 5 opens the list of vouchers behind it, and a saved voucher lists the figures it is part of (the way back, below). `lib/domain/figure-vouchers.ts` holds the rules, pure and tested; `hooks/use-figure-drill.ts` builds the lists for the views.

- **A figure is described by a contribution**: what one entry adds to it, or null when the entry takes no part. `figureRows` lists the entries that add something, in date and voucher order, and a zero is left out.
- **VAT figures reuse `vatReport` on one entry**, so the vouchers add up to the period's figure exactly, rounding included. That covers the rate tables, the other-sales and EU-purchase rows, the banner and every field of the return (`vatFieldSelector`). 308 is the signed tax due, so a refund's vouchers carry a minus.
- **Statement figures read the same lines and signs the statement does.** A line, a section, revenue (316), deductible expenses (355) and the result (358, 359; a loss is turned over so it adds up to the positive figure). The entries are the tax basis (§6.3), so an entry sits on its payment date and an open one is absent, as on the statement.
- **Form 5's fields map to those lines** by account (`form5Selection`). A portion (320, 322, 328, 338, 342, 347) lists its parent's vouchers in full, and the dialog says the figure counts only part of them. 388 and 392 map to the two 364 lines.
- **Seven balance fields have vouchers** (`balanceContribution`): 711 (open sales on 31.12., gross), 749 (open purchases), 725 (open purchases and the loan movements up to the year's end), 394 (wages by payment date) and the pool's 801, 802 and 804. 725 and 749 also hold debts typed in by hand and the loan's opening balance, which no voucher stands behind, so their list falls short and says so. KPL 2:6 covers what is made from the books, so a figure typed in by hand is outside it. The typed-in fields (706, 708, 709, 714, 716), the totals 718, 733 and 734, 750 and the closing pool (704, 806) have no list and show no link.
- **The list is `FigureDialog`**, read-only, on top of whatever is open, so a VAT return or Form 5 dialog keeps its step. `figure` in the store is not persisted and is cleared by `openDialog`, `closeDialog` and a new or closed book. A row opens the voucher in the entry dialog, which closes both. The rows are those of the click, and the dialog is modal.
- **The vouchers' sum is shown against the figure** when they differ, with the reason. A row shows the payment date when it differs from the voucher's, or "avoin".
- **In the tax forms a separate list button sits beside the amount**, since the amount is the copy button. Elsewhere the amount is a `FigureLink`: dotted underline on screen, plain on paper. A figure of zero has none.
- **The way back** is `entryFigureRefs`: the VAT return fields, Form 5 fields and balance fields a voucher adds to, found with the same contributions as the lists, so a voucher is listed under a figure exactly when that figure's list holds it. The VAT return is the one of the filing period that holds the voucher's date. Form 5 reads the voucher on its payment date and not at all while it is open (§6.3). The balance fields are asked for the voucher's own years and the selected year. The totals (308, 316, 355, 358, 359) follow from the fields and are left out. The entry dialog shows it as "Missä tosite näkyy" for a saved voucher (`EntryFigures`), in a closed section like the history, by the voucher as saved and not by the form being edited, and a line opens the list behind that field. A voucher that counts nowhere, such as a VAT payment, shows nothing.
- `lib/domain/figure-vouchers.test.ts` checks that every field of the VAT return and every statement line adds up and that the two directions agree, and `test/figure-drill.test.tsx` the views, the entry dialog and the stacking.

### 8.19 Archived years (Arkistoidut vuodet)

`lib/domain/year-archive.ts` (pure, tested), `archiveClosedYear` in `lib/commands/io-commands.ts` and `components/app/archive-banner.tsx`. A closed year can leave the working book and live in a ZIP of its own, so a book of ten years stays small. The archive opens read-only as a reference.

- **Who can be archived** (`archiveBlockers`). The year has ended, its Form 5 is marked as filed (§8.8), every closed VAT period of it is marked as filed (§6.5), no older year is still in the book (years go oldest first), no voucher dated in it is open or was paid after it, and an asset opening balance is not dated beyond the next year. The overview's checklist row for a closed year (§8.12) has an "Arkistoi" button beside "Tallessa", and the blockers are listed in a dialog when any holds.
- **What moves.** The vouchers dated and paid in the year (`movableEntries`) and their receipts. A voucher dated in the year but paid later, and one dated later but paid in the year, stay in the working book under their own dates, since a later year's result and open items read them (§6.3). The archive file holds both kinds as copies (`archiveContents`), so its income statement and Form 5 read as filed.
- **What carries forward.** The asset pool's closing balance and the loans outstanding are written as the next year's opening (`rolledOpenings`, §8.8, §6.8), and a book with no start month gets the first entry's month, so the VAT timeline does not move. The year-end figures, the filed marks and the change log stay in the working book's `settings.json`.
- **The archive is a bookkeeping ZIP** (§7.2): a CSV, receipts and a `settings.json` that holds only the year's marks, its year-end figures and its change log records, and `archiveOf` (the year). It has the book's export language, its password if the book is encrypted (§7.8), and an integrity seal (§7.9). Its `yearsArchived` mark locks every voucher of the year (§8.11).
- **Safety order.** The save picker comes first, so only a browser that writes files directly can archive (§7.5). The ZIP is built, refused if any receipt is missing from this browser, read back in memory, and written. Only then do the entries leave the book (`applyArchive`), which marks it dirty. A failure at any step leaves the book as it was. The working book's file on disk still holds the year until the user saves, and the archive holds it too.
- **The working book keeps a stub** per year in `archives` (`settings.json`): the file name, the date and the entry count. Asetukset, Yleiset lists them. The app cannot find the file again, since a browser hands back no path, so the user opens it from wherever they put it.
- **Opening an archive** is opening any file (§7.2). `archiveOf` in its settings sets `archiveYear` in the store, which is persisted. Nothing is parked: the archive replaces the open book after the usual unsaved-changes prompt (§8.11), and "Sulje arkisto" in the banner returns to the landing page, where the working book is opened again from its file.
- **Read-only is the store's rule, not each action's.** While `archiveYear` is set, a state change that touches any of `BOOK_DATA_KEYS` is dropped, so no action needs its own check. Filters, the selected year and sorting stay free. `openDialog` opens only the dialogs that show (a viewer, export, the VAT return, the year-end dialog, the book check of §8.23, and an existing voucher). Saving, autosave and "Uusi tosite" are refused or disabled. A download of the archive is allowed and keeps `archiveOf`.
- **The confirmation can also make a retention package** (§8.21): its second button, "Arkistoi ja tee säilytyspaketti", builds the package from the archive ZIP before anything is written, and downloads it once the archive is on disk.
- **Not handled:** unarchiving into the working book, parking the working book while an archive is open, archiving a year with open items, and finding an archive again from its stub.
- `lib/domain/year-archive.test.ts` covers the rules and `stores/archive-guard.test.ts` the read-only guard.

### 8.20 Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z)

`lib/domain/history.ts` (pure, tested), `stores/history-store.ts`, `lib/commands/history-commands.ts` and the shortcut in `hooks/use-app-shortcuts.ts`. It goes beyond the 10-second toasts of §8.11: any change to the entries or the categories since the last save can be stepped back, and forward again.

- **A step is a snapshot of `entries`, `cats` and `invoices`.** `startRecordingHistory` subscribes to the book store, mounted in `AppShell` for the tab that holds the bookkeeping (§12). Each change pushes the state before it on the undo stack and empties the redo stack. Writes in one task count as one step, so a split or a bank import undoes in one press. Settings, filed marks, recurring templates (of entries and of invoices), the product register, category rules and the year-end figures are not part of it.
- **The toasts stay as they are.** A toast's "Kumoa" writes to the store like any other change, so it is one more step: Ctrl+Z after it undoes the undo.
- **The stacks drop** on a save (`dirty` falls), when another bookkeeping takes over (`bookEpoch`), and when a year is archived, whose entries cannot come back without the stub that says where they went (§8.19). They live in memory only. With autosave on, a save follows every change within seconds, so the stack is short.
- **Locks hold** (§8.11). A step that would add, remove or change a voucher that `lockedEntries` finds locked, in its current or its restored version, is refused with a toast and stays on its stack. A step may still add to a filed return through an entry the return did not count before, and does not ask the way a save does.
- **Receipts the stacks can bring back stay in IndexedDB** (`historyAttachmentIds`, read by `sweepReceipts`). Clearing the stacks sets the sweep off.
- **Voucher numbers come back as they were.** `restoreSnapshot` writes the entries as they are and logs the difference in the change log: a delete, an edit or a restore per voucher.
- **Keys.** `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`Z` undoes, `Ctrl`/`⌘`+`Shift`+`Z` and `Ctrl`+`Y` redo. In a field the browser's own undo keeps the key, and nothing fires behind a dialog or confirmation, during an import or a save, in an archive or with no bookkeeping open.
- No cap on the number of steps. Entries are shared between snapshots, so a step costs one array.
- `lib/domain/history.test.ts`, `stores/history-store.test.ts` and `test/app-shortcuts.test.tsx` cover it.

### 8.21 Year archive package (Säilytyspaketti)

`lib/domain/year-reports.ts`, `lib/domain/retention-package.ts` (both pure and tested), `lib/browser/report-pdf.ts` and `lib/commands/retention-commands.ts`. KPL 2:10 has the books kept for ten years, and a trader who wants a copy that reads without the app needs more than the working ZIP. The package is one ZIP for one year:

```
kirjanpito-2026-sailytyspaketti.zip      books-2026-retention-package.zip
  README.txt                               README.txt
  hashes.txt                               hashes.txt
  receipt-hashes.txt                       receipt-hashes.txt
  kirjanpito-2026.zip                      books-2026.zip
  raportit/                                reports/
    1 - Päiväkirja 2026.pdf                  1 - Journal 2026.pdf
    2 - Pääkirja 2026.pdf                    2 - General ledger 2026.pdf
    3 - Tililuettelo 2026.pdf                3 - Account list 2026.pdf
    4 - Tuloslaskelma 2026.pdf               4 - Income statement 2026.pdf
    5 - Lomake 5 2026.pdf                    5 - Form 5 2026.pdf
    6 - Arvonlisäveroilmoitus ...pdf         6 - VAT return ...pdf
```

- **Where it comes from.** Two entries, one code path. The overview's checklist row for a closed year and the archive banner have "Säilytyspaketti", which downloads it for any year with vouchers and changes nothing in the book (`exportRetentionPackage`). The year archive of §8.19 offers it as the second button of its confirmation (`makeRetentionPackage`). The standalone export needs no file picker, so it works in every browser.
- **The year's ZIP inside it** is built the way the archive's is (`archiveSettings`, `archiveContents`), so it opens read-only as an archive of that year. In the archive flow it is the archive file itself.
- **The reports are drawn, not printed.** A browser cannot write its print dialog's PDF to a file, and the package needs the bytes to hash them. `yearReports` turns the journal, general ledger (with its reconciliation to the statement), account list (with the list of books of KPL 2:7a), income statement and Form 5 into a layout-neutral document of tables and text, from the same domain functions the views use. `renderReportPdf` draws it with pdf-lib in the standard fonts, so the text is selectable and `toWinAnsi` applies (§8.8). The look is plainer than the sheets on screen. The statement and Form 5 read the whole book by payment year (§6.3), so they are made from all entries, not only the year's.
- **The VAT returns are the click-through on paper** (`vatReturnReports`). KPL 2:6 asks for a trace from a return to its vouchers, and §8.18 exists only inside the app. Each filing period of the year (§6.5) gets a PDF numbered after the five reports: the return's fields as OmaVero asks for them, then under each non-zero field the vouchers it is made of, found with the same contributions as the click-through, so they add up to the field. 308 is the signed balance, so a refund's vouchers add up to a negative amount, and the note says so. The due date and the filed mark are in the header. A filed return whose figures have changed since carries a page listing the fields and both amounts (§6.5). A period is left out when it ended before the book began, and when it has neither a voucher on the return nor a filed mark. The filing period is the book's setting, so a book that changed it during the year gets the year's periods in the current kind only.
- **The reports follow the book's export language** (§7.1), like the other names in the file. The README and the folder names do too.
- **Two hash lists, both in `sha256sum` form** (the hash, two spaces, the path). `hashes.txt` covers every file in the package but itself. `receipt-hashes.txt` lists each receipt in the year's ZIP by the path it has once that ZIP is unpacked. The receipts are read back out of the finished ZIP, so the list describes the file and not what the book meant to put in it. The README says how to check both.
- **The hashes catch accidents and prove nothing else**, as the integrity seal does (§7.9). Anyone who changes a file on purpose can recompute them.
- **An encrypted book keeps its year ZIP encrypted** (§7.8). The reports, the lists and the README are plain, because the package has to open in any tool years from now, so the standalone export asks first and says so. The outer ZIP is never encrypted.
- **A receipt the browser and the file being replaced both lack** stops the standalone export, as it stops the archive (`io.archiveLost`).
- **Not handled:** a month's receipts stamped with voucher numbers for an accountant (a separate idea), encrypting the reports, signing the hash list, and a package for several years at once.
- `lib/domain/year-reports.test.ts` covers the §10 figures in the reports, the PDF render, the hash lists and the ZIP.

### 8.22 Tax calendar (Kalenteri)

`lib/domain/tax-calendar.ts` (pure, tested), `lib/i18n/calendar-label.ts` and `components/calendar/calendar-view.tsx`, at `/app/calendar`. One list of the selected year's due dates, oldest first, and an `.ics` file of them. The last periods of the year before are in it when they fall due in this one.

- **Dates the app works out itself.** The VAT returns of the filing period (§6.5), from the period that holds the book's start; a return marked as filed shows "Ilmoitettu". The EC Sales List (§8.7), for the months that have EU sales only, due on the 20th of the next month. Form 5 of the year before, on Verohallinto's date in `data/rules.json` (§6.11), "Ilmoitettu" once the year is marked (§8.8). A year with no row in that table lists no Form 5 date.
- **Dates the user picks the months for.** The YEL contribution and advance tax depend on the pension company's invoice and on the advance tax decision, which the app cannot see. `payMonths` (`settings.json`, dirty like any setting) holds the months of each, chosen in Asetukset, Yleiset, and the calendar links there. YEL falls due on the 20th (Elo; Varma's July date is the 20th too) and advance tax on the 23rd (vero.fi), both moved to the next working day (`nextWorkingDay`, §6.5). Pension companies allow 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12 instalments a year. Verohallinto sets 2, 3, 6 or 12 advance tax instalments by its size (170 to 500 €: March and September; over 500 to 1 700 €: February, July and November; over 1 700 to 10 000 €: every second month from February; over 10 000 €: monthly). The app suggests none of this and tells the user to check the dates.
- **Advance tax amounts and payments.** `lib/domain/advance-tax.ts`. The user types the year's total from the advance tax decision in Asetukset, Yleiset ("Ennakkoveropäätös {year}"), kept per year in `payMonths.advanceTaxYearly` (cents). The calendar splits it evenly over the chosen months, with the cents that do not divide on the last one, and shows each amount. An instalment is paid when a line 365 expense (`365`, §5) is dated in its month, so a booking made by hand or by a bank import rule counts. A due instalment with an amount and no payment has "Kirjaa maksetuksi", which adds that expense on the due date through `addEntriesWithUndo` (§8.11). Nothing is stored for paid status, so deleting the entry makes the instalment open again.
- **The estimate.** With a municipal rate in the tax details (§6.14), the panel above the list shows the tax the year's profit (358) adds on the year's scale, and the gap to the decision. Without one, but with `payMonths.taxRate` set, it is the profit (zero for a loss) times that flat rate, the user's own guess. With neither there is no estimate.
- **The export** (`toIcs`) is an iCalendar file of all-day events with a stable `UID` per date, so importing it again updates the events instead of doubling them, and a reminder the day before. It has no network part and no link back to the app.
- **Not handled:** instalments of tax after assessment, payroll dates, and a due date moved by a decision on one user's case.
- `lib/domain/tax-calendar.test.ts` and `test/calendar-view.test.tsx` cover it.

### 8.23 Check the book (Tarkista kirjanpito)

`lib/domain/book-check.ts` (pure, tested) and `components/check/book-check-dialog.tsx`, opened from a row in the file menu (§8.2, `DialogState` kind `check-book`). It runs the rule sets of §6.10 over the whole book and lists what they find, grouped by year. It is the one place that answers "is anything wrong anywhere", where the overview's checklist (§8.12) and the year-end dialog (§8.8) each look at the selected year.

- **It holds no rule.** `bookCheck` builds each set's context and calls `evaluate`: the year-end set (`YEAR_END_RULES`, which carries the entry checks of §8.4) for every year that has entries, `FORM5_RULES` over the Form 5 figures of every year, and `BOOK_LIMIT_RULES` and `VAT_LIMIT_RULES` once for the book. A new rule in any of those sets shows up here with no change, apart from its wording.
- **Left out on purpose.** The VAT return's set (`VAT_CHECK_RULES`) repeats the year-end set's entry checks, missing files and unfiled periods for one period. The period locks (`LOCK_RULES`) describe a voucher's state, not a fault. Two year-end rules ask whether a year may be locked (`yearOngoing`, `noEntries`), which a check of the book does not do. `unsaved` is reported once, against no year.
- **A year that has not ended gets no close-of-year findings.** The year's missing year-end figures, its unused depreciation and its open items at 31.12. mean nothing until the year is over. Its entry checks, missing files, unfiled periods and empty months still run.
- **Order.** The book's own findings come first, then each year newest first. Blockers lead within each group, then warnings, then information.
- **The Form 5 rules check the app's own figures.** A breach there means the figures the app worked out disagree with Verohallinto's check rules (§6.10), so the row says to check them in OmaVero before copying.
- **A way in** (the entry checks' "Näytä", the unfiled VAT periods, the depreciation register, the year-end figures) selects the finding's year first, closes the dialog and opens the view. The wording and the ways in are the year-end dialog's (`useDescribeYearEnd`), and the limit rules' wording is the overview's (`limit-text.ts`), so one rule reads the same everywhere.
- **It also opens on an archive** (§8.19), since it only reads. Nothing is stored.
- `lib/domain/book-check.test.ts` and `test/book-check.test.tsx` cover it.

### 8.25 Audit package (Tarkastuspaketti)

`lib/domain/audit-range.ts`, `lib/domain/retention-package.ts`, `lib/domain/year-reports.ts` (`auditReports`), `lib/commands/audit-commands.ts` and `components/export/audit-dialog.tsx`. The retention package of §8.21 is for one closed year. An audit (Verohallinto, an auditor, an accountant) asks for a stretch of days that rarely matches a year, so this is the same package with a free range in place of a year.

- **Where it starts.** "Tarkastuspaketti" in the file menu (§8.1) opens `AuditDialog` (`DialogState` kind `audit-package`, allowed in an archive book). It has a start and an end date, quick picks (this year, last year, the quarter before the current one), the number of vouchers in the range and a download button, disabled for a range out of order or with no vouchers. `exportAuditPackage` does the rest and changes nothing in the book.
- **The range selects by voucher date.** `auditEntries` keeps the entries dated from the start to the end, both days included. A voucher paid in the range and dated outside it is not in the package, and neither is a later year's carried balance.
- **The contents are the retention package's** with the year's reports left out: the reports as PDFs in `raportit/` or `reports/`, the book ZIP, `hashes.txt`, `receipt-hashes.txt` and a README, all built by the same `assemble` function. The book ZIP is an ordinary book, not an archive (`auditSettings`): it holds the range's vouchers and attachments, the VAT marks of periods wholly inside the range and the change log records dated in it, and it does not open read-only.
- **The reports** (`auditReports`): the journal, the general ledger and the account list over the range, then one VAT return per filing period that lies wholly inside it, with the vouchers behind every field as in §8.21. A period cut by the range is left out, since a return for part of a period was never filed. The books take a `BookScope`, a year or a `DateRange` (`lib/domain/books.ts`), so a year's reports are the same functions with a year. A journal over a range names the year in each month heading. The ledger has no reconciliation to a statement, which exists for a year only.
- **No income statement and no Form 5.** Both are worked out for a whole year and by payment date (§6.3), so a range has no honest figure for them. The README says they are in the year's retention package.
- **The file is named** `kirjanpito-2026-01-01_2026-06-30-tarkastuspaketti.zip`, or `…-audit-package.zip` for an English book, from the file's name and the range.
- **Encryption, receipts and hashes behave as in §8.21.** An encrypted book keeps its ZIP encrypted while the reports stay plain, and the dialog asks first. A receipt the browser lacks stops the export. The hashes catch accidents and prove nothing else.
- `lib/domain/audit-package.test.ts` covers the range books, the reports, the file list and the quick picks.

### 8.27 Sales invoices (Laskut)

Built, apart from what the last bullet lists and the year archive and audit books, which do not carry invoices yet: the reference number, barcode, QR text, data files, row maths, the issue command, credit notes, sending, the PDF, the pages and the dialogs (`invoice-reference.ts`, `invoice-barcode.ts`, `code128.ts`, `invoice-epc.ts`, `invoice-file.ts`, `invoice-calc.ts`, `invoice-status.ts`, `invoice-input.ts`, `invoice-labels.ts`, `invoice-mail.ts`, `lib/browser/share.ts`, `lib/commands/invoice-commands.ts`, `lib/browser/invoice-pdf.ts`, `components/invoices/`; the book store holds `invoiceSettings`, `invoices` and `customers`). Before this section the app only recorded invoices. It now writes them: create one, number it, print it as a PDF a customer can pay from, and book it. The bank import (§8.16) closes it when the payment arrives.

- **An invoice is a record that owns entries.** `Invoice` (`invoices.json`, §7.6) holds the number, customer, dates, rows, reference, language and the ids of its entries. The entries stay ordinary entries (§4), so the VAT return, the income statement, the ledger and `balancesAt` need no change. The link lives in the invoice, not in the CSV, so the CSV columns do not change.
- **Booked on issue, as open entries.** A draft is editable and has no entry. Issuing locks the invoice and creates the entries: income, dated on the invoice date, `paidOn: null`, described "Lasku 1001 – customer". When to count the income for tax stays with §6.3, so a cash-basis trader reports it in the year of payment and an accrual trader by the invoice date. Issuing is undoable (§8.20). It downloads nothing, and the number is released by an undo only while it is the newest.
- **Rows.** Each row has a description, quantity, unit, unit price without VAT, a VAT rate and an income category. Rows are grouped by category and rate, and each group becomes one entry in `net` mode. Groups of one invoice share a `split` id. VAT is rounded once per rate group, and the printed VAT line is the one `calcEntry` gives for that entry; a test holds the two equal. A choice of per-row rounding is not offered.
- **Numbering.** One continuous series across years, set by a "first number" in the invoice settings and then the highest used number plus one. A number is never reused or renumbered. A draft takes no number until it is issued. Numbers are only ever added to the highest, apart from a reopened draft taking its own back. A gap comes from deleting an issued invoice (below), and a duplicate only from editing `invoices.json` outside the app, which the integrity seal (§7.9) reports.
- **Seller settings** (`invoiceSettings`): street address, postcode and city, email, IBAN, payment term in days (default 14), a free-text term for fi and en, and the late-payment interest shown, the reminder fee in cents and an optional interest rate in percent that replaces the statutory one on a reminder. The business name, ID and phone come from the existing settings. Issuing is refused with a plain message while the address or IBAN is missing. Only a Finnish IBAN can carry the virtual barcode.
- **Customers** (`customers.json`, §7.6): name, address, country, VAT ID, email, invoice language (fi or en) and default payment term. A customer is picked from the register or typed once and saved. An issued invoice keeps a copy of the customer's details as issued, so editing the register never changes an old invoice. Export and import of the register is a separate JSON or CSV file, for moving customers to another book.
- **A repeated invoice is hinted.** While a draft is open, a warning line replaces the footer hint when an issued invoice (not a credit note, not one a credit note has cancelled) has the same customer (the register id, or the name as `normalizeName` reads it), the same rows (description, quantity, unit price and VAT rate, in any order) and an invoice date within seven days (`duplicateInvoice`, `NEAR_INVOICE_DAYS`). It never stops issuing: a monthly invoice with the same rows is a month apart and does not match.
- **Reference number.** `invoice-reference.ts` builds the Finnish viitenumero from the invoice number with the 7-3-1 check digit, and the RF creditor reference as an option. It is the one field the bank import matches on.
- **Virtual barcode.** `invoice-barcode.ts` builds the 54-digit string: version 4 for a Finnish reference (IBAN digits, euros and cents, a zero reserve, the reference padded to 20, the due date as YYMMDD) and version 5 for an RF reference. It is drawn as Code 128-C bars in the PDF. An amount above 999 999,99 € or a non-Finnish IBAN leaves the barcode out and says so. The layout is checked against Finanssiala's published specification before the code is written.
- **QR code.** `invoice-epc.ts` builds the EPC SEPA payload (service tag BCD, version 002, SCT) with the reference in the remittance text. The QR matrix comes from a small dependency imported dynamically, like `pdf-lib`.
- **The PDF** (`invoice-pdf.ts`, `pdf-lib`, loaded when an invoice is issued or previewed). It carries the fields the VAT Act requires on an invoice, the payment slip with IBAN, reference, due date, barcode and QR, the late-interest line and the free-text term, in the customer's language. The PDF is stored as an attachment named "Lasku 1001 - Customer.pdf", and the same stored file is attached to every entry of the invoice (§7.2). The required fields are checked against vero.fi when the layout is made.
- **Status is worked out, not stored.** Draft until issued. Then open while any entry has `paidOn: null`, overdue once the due date has passed, paid when every entry has a payment date, and credited when a credit note names it. Only `issuedAt` and the credit link are stored.
- **An issued invoice is not edited.** Its entries carry a lock of the kind `invoice` (`period-lock.ts`, §8.11), the same lock a filed period gives, so the entry dialog shows them read-only and says which invoice they belong to, and the ledger, the bulk actions and delete refuse them. The payment date stays editable. The lock is read from the book's invoices, so an entry is locked from the moment its invoice is issued. A mistake is corrected with a credit note. An issued invoice can also be deleted (`deleteIssuedInvoice`): "Poista lasku" asks first, since the number is not reused and the series gets a gap unless it was the newest, then removes the invoice and its entries. A credit note, and an invoice with an issued credit note, cannot be deleted, because the credit settles against it; a stored year or a filed return still holds the entries (§8.11). The toast and Ctrl+Z (§8.20) bring back the invoice and its entries; the history snapshot carries `invoices`, and only an invoice unchanged by a step locks its entries (`unchangedInvoices`), so a step that issues, undoes or deletes it is not refused by its own lock. A draft can be deleted as before. **Reopen** (`reopenIssuedInvoice`, "Avaa muokattavaksi"): the same invoices are refused as for a delete, and it asks first. It removes the entries and turns the invoice back into a draft that keeps its number as `reservedNumber` (`invoices.json` keeps it on a draft only). `nextInvoiceNumber` counts a reserved number as used, so nothing else takes it, and `buildIssue` gives it back when the draft is issued again, with a new reference, seller copy, entries and PDF. A customer who already received the old version is the user's to tell; the dialog says so. The toast undo restores the issued invoice unless the draft has been changed since, and Ctrl+Z (§8.20) restores it as a history step.
- **Credit note.** "Hyvitä" on an issued invoice starts a draft credit note (`startCreditNote`, `creditDraft`): the same customer and language and the same rows with their quantities turned negative, dated today, with no due date. The user can remove rows or shrink quantities to credit part. It takes a number from the same series and is issued like an invoice, but `issueProblems` wants every quantity negative, a negative total, an issued invoice to credit and no more than what is left to credit (`creditableAmount`, the invoice's gross less the issued credit notes that name it). Its entries are negative open income entries, so VAT, the income statement and the ledger follow without a new rule, rounded as the exact opposite of the sale (`roundCents`), and each points at the invoice's entry of the same category and rate with `reverses` (§8.4). Its PDF is headed "HYVITYSLASKU" and names the credited invoice, and has no due date, reference or payment slip.
- **A credit that cancels an unpaid invoice settles both sides.** When a credit note takes the whole of an invoice none of whose entries is paid, the invoice's entries are marked paid on the credit's date and the credit's own on theirs, so the two net to nothing whichever way the trader is taxed (§6.3). This is skipped if the credit's date is before the invoice's or the year's Form 5 is filed. A part credit, or a credit of an invoice already paid, leaves the credit entries open until the user marks them paid when the money is returned or set off. One toast undoes the whole issue, the settlement included. The status of a credit note is "Hyvitys" and an invoice the credit notes have taken in full is "Hyvitetty".
- **Payment, whole or in part.** `recordInvoicePayment` takes the amount received and the day. The invoice's unpaid entries are settled in book order (`applyInvoicePayment`): an entry the money covers is marked paid, and the one it reaches only in part is split in two, a new paid entry for the gross that arrived and the original reduced to what is still owed, in the entry's own mode (an invoice's entries are net, so the paid part is the net whose gross is nearest what arrived and the VAT of the two parts can differ from the whole by a cent). The paid part is a copy of the entry without the `split` link, since parts of a split share their payment (§8.5), with the same files, and the invoice's `entryIds` take it, so it keeps the invoice lock. This way income and VAT follow the real payment dates for a trader taxed on payment (§6.3). An amount over what is owed is refused. One toast undoes the whole payment, the split included. "Merkitse maksetuksi" asks for the amount (the whole debt to start with) and the day, and the list shows "Avoinna" under the total of a partly paid invoice. In the bank import a row whose reference is an open invoice's and whose amount equals the open total proposes the invoice as paid whole; a row with less proposes a part payment (status "Osamaksu", `partial` on the proposal), which books only that amount, several rows of one statement being applied in turn.
- **Sending** (`sendInvoice`, `lib/browser/share.ts`, `lib/domain/invoice-mail.ts`). "Lähetä" on an issued invoice or credit note, in the list and in the invoice dialog, draws the PDF and hands it to the share sheet where `navigator.canShare` accepts files, with a subject ("Lasku 1001 – seller") and a text in the invoice's language. The text has the amount and due date and, on an invoice, the account and reference number, so the customer can pay from the mail alone. A cancelled share does nothing more. Where there is no share sheet for files, or it fails, the PDF is downloaded and a `mailto:` link opens the user's mail program on the customer's address (empty when the register has none) with the same subject and text, and a toast says to attach the file. The app sends nothing itself, and a draft cannot be sent.
- **Locks** (§8.11). An invoice dated in a filed VAT period or a year whose Form 5 is filed behaves like a new entry there: the same warning or refusal, applied when it is issued.
- **Archives** (§8.19). Archiving a year needs all its entries paid in it, so the year's invoices are settled when they leave. The working book keeps its invoices and customers, which keeps the number series going and a credit note able to name an old invoice, and an invoice whose entries are archived reads as paid. The archive ZIP and an audit or retention package carry only the invoices that own an entry they hold, and the customers those invoices were made for (`invoicesOfEntries`).
- **Where it is in the app.** "Laskut" in the sidebar at `/app/invoices` lists the invoices, drafts first and then newest number first, each with a status pill, the gross amount and the actions open or view, PDF, reminder (when overdue), mark as paid and delete a draft. "Osapuolet" at `/app/invoices/parties` is the counterparty register (§8.29), customers and suppliers together; the invoice dialog offers customers only. The seller details, the reminder fee and the optional interest rate are on the "Laskutus" tab of Asetukset at `/app/settings/invoicing`, and each field commits on blur. `DialogState` has the kinds `invoice` (a new draft, a draft or an issued invoice, read-only once issued) and `customer`. The invoice dialog fills the due date from the customer's term or the book's, until the user sets one, and offers only enabled domestic income categories.
- **Receivables** (`invoice-aging.ts`, "Saatavat" at `/app/invoices/aging`). Every issued invoice or credit note with something unpaid, by customer and by how late: not yet due (the due day itself counts), 1–14, 15–30, 31–60 and over 60 days past the due date, with totals. The amount is the gross of the unpaid entries, so a part payment leaves only what is owed; an unsettled credit note counts as a negative amount and nets against its customer. A customer's row opens to its invoices, each opening the invoice. It is worked out each time and never stored.
- **Payment reminders** (`invoice-reminder.ts`, `renderReminderPdf`). "Maksumuistutus" on an overdue invoice asks for the day, the late-payment rate and the fee, and shows what the customer will be asked to pay: the open balance, simple interest on it for the whole days from the due date to the reminder (rate in percent a year, on 365 days, `lateInterest`) and the fee, with a new due date seven days later. The rate is the seller's own (`interestRate` in the invoice settings) or else the statutory general rate on that day, which comes from the `lateInterestRate` limit in the rule tables (§6.11): the reference rate of the Interest Act plus seven points, set for each half-year. A day before the first stored rate has none, and the dialog asks for a rate instead of guessing. The fee comes from `reminderFee` in the settings (500 cents to start with). The letter is a PDF in the invoice's language with the seller, buyer, figures and the invoice's payment slip for the total and the new due date; it goes to the share sheet or is downloaded with a mail to the customer, as an invoice does. It is not booked and not attached to any entry: the interest and the fee become income when the user records them when the money comes. Each reminder is kept on the invoice (`reminders` in `invoices.json`: date, balance, rate, interest, fee, new due date), the list shows how many were made and when the last one was, and the toast undoes the record. One level only; a second letter is simply another reminder.
- **Copy an invoice** (`copyInvoice`, `copyDraft`, `invoice-draft.ts`). "Kopioi uudeksi" on any invoice that is not a credit note, in the list and in the dialog of an issued one, makes a new draft dated today with the same rows, note and language. The customer's details come from the register as they are now when the invoice names a register customer, and the due date follows that customer's term or the book's. The supply period, number, reminders and credit link stay with the original, and a VAT rate that has changed since follows the new date (§5). A credit note is not copied, since its rows only make sense against the invoice it credits.
- **Product register** (`Product`, `invoiceSettings.products` in `settings.json`, "Tuotteet" at `/app/invoices/products`). A product has a name, unit, unit price without VAT, VAT rate and income category, the fields of an invoice row. "Lisää tuote…" beside "Lisää rivi" in the invoice dialog fills the last row while nothing is typed in it and adds a row otherwise (`productRow`), with the rate moved to the invoice's date. A typed row whose description no product has can be kept as a product with the bookmark button on the row (`productFromRow`). The row stays editable after it is filled, and changing or removing a product leaves the invoices made from it alone. A credit note offers no products.
- **Recurring invoices** (`RecurringInvoice`, `invoiceSettings.recurring` in `settings.json`, "Toistuvat" at `/app/invoices/recurring`). "Toista laskua" on an invoice (not a credit note) makes a template with its customer, language, rows and note, an interval of 1, 2, 3, 6 or 12 months (`RECURRING_EVERY`), the date of the next invoice and an optional last date. The next date starts at the first date on the schedule after today (`firstRecurringDate`), so nothing past is made unless the user sets an earlier one. The day of the month comes from that date, and a shorter month uses its last day (`nextRecurringDate`). Nothing is issued by itself: there is no backend, so when the app opens, at midnight in an open tab and whenever the templates change, `useRecurringInvoices` runs `makeRecurringDrafts`, which turns each date that has come into a draft dated that day and linked by `recurring` (`invoices.json`), and moves the template's next date on (`recurringDrafts`). A book left closed makes at most twelve drafts per template at once (`MAX_CATCH_UP`), the latest ones. A paused template, a date after the last one and an archive book make none. Each draft takes the register customer's current details and term, and the rate valid on its date. A toast says how many were made, the list marks such a draft "Toistuvasta laskusta", and the overview's checklist counts the ones not issued yet (rule `recurringDrafts`) with a link to the list. Making them is not an undo step (`withoutHistory`), since the templates have already moved past their dates. The template's list has the interval, next date or "Keskeytetty" / "Päättynyt", the total and how many invoices it has made; the edit dialog changes the interval, next date, last date and the pause, and the rows are changed by making a new template from an invoice that has the new rows. Deleting a template has a toast undo and leaves the drafts it made.
- **Not built:** Finvoice or PEPPOL XML, quotes, and EU or reverse-charge invoices (domestic income categories only). Each is a later change.

### 8.28 Stocktake and stock valuation (Varasto)

For a trader who holds stock. Form 5 asks for the stock at the end of the year on 708 (goods) and 709 (materials, supplies and self-made products), and the year's change goes on line 333 (§8.8). This replaces a typed total with a counted list, and the list is also the voucher the change rests on.

- **Where.** A "Varasto" tab under Tuloslaskelma, at `/app/statement/inventory` (`components/inventory/inventory-view.tsx`). The list is for the selected year and describes the stock on 31.12.
- **A row** (`StockItem`, `lib/domain/inventory.ts`): name, the trader's own code, unit, type (goods or materials), the quantity counted, the cost of one unit, optional purchase layers (§6.13), and an optional lower value per unit with the reason for it. Quantities are integer thousandths and unit costs integer cents, like the invoice rows. A row with a quantity of zero is not counted.
- **The page** shows the cost method, the value of the stock, the rows counted, the cost, the write-down, and the 708 and 709 totals. The totals reach Form 5 by themselves (below). A table below holds the rows, which are edited in place: a field commits on blur or Enter, and text that cannot be read puts the old value back. A zero quantity or cost shows as an empty field, so typing never lands after a leading zero. New rows go to the top, and the table has a search by name or code and the page-size control of the ledger.
- **Purchase layers** open in a dialog per row. A row with none uses its unit cost for every unit, which is all most traders need.
- **Import and export** use a semicolon CSV with a byte order mark (`lib/domain/inventory-csv.ts`). The header is read in Finnish or English with a few common spreadsheet names (Tuote, Kpl, Hankintahinta), and a row with no name or no readable quantity or cost is skipped and counted. The CSV carries no purchase layers. Imported rows are added to the list, never replace it.
- **Start from last year.** When a year has no list and the year before does, a button carries the rows still in stock over (`carryOver`). Under FIFO the layers are cut down to the units left. Under average cost they fold into one unit cost. The lower value and its reason are dropped, since they are a judgement about one year-end.
- **The stocktake list as a document.** "Lataa inventaariolista" writes a PDF through the report writer of §8.21 (`stocktakeReport`, `lib/domain/inventory-report.ts`): every counted row with its cost, write-down and value, then the totals for 708 and 709. "Kirjaa varaston muutos" on Varat ja velat attaches it to the entry beside the memo voucher of §8.8, so the figures rest on the list they came from.
- **The list sets 708 and 709.** `syncYearEnd` (`lib/domain/inventory.ts`) writes the goods total to `goods` and the materials total to `otherCurrent` of that year's `yearEnd` whenever the list or the cost method changes and when a book is opened. It is the one place the list reaches the figures, so Form 5, the reports, the new year and the book check read them as before. A year with no counted rows keeps its typed figures, and the two fields in the year-end dialog (§8.8) are read-only with a note while the year has counted rows. Emptying the list unlocks them and leaves the last figures in place. A year whose Form 5 is filed is never rewritten.
- **Locks.** A year whose Form 5 is marked as filed (§8.11) shows the list read-only, and the cost method is locked with it.
- **Stored** in `inventory.json` (§7.6): the method and the rows by year. A book with no rows writes no file.
- **Cost of goods sold and margin.** A second row of cards under the totals shows the year's revenue, purchases on line 333, the cost of goods sold, the gross margin, the margin as a share of revenue and the days of stock (`goodsMargin`, `lib/domain/inventory.ts`). The cost of goods sold is opening stock plus purchases less closing stock, with the stock taken from the year-end figures (708 plus 709) of the year and the year before. Purchases are line 333 less the booked stock-change entry, found by its description, so the figure is the same before and after that entry is booked. Revenue is the taxable revenue of the income statement on the book's tax basis (§6.3). The cards show only when the year has purchases or a stock figure.
- **Findings.** Three year-end rules (`lib/domain/year-end.ts`, §6.10) feed the year-end dialog (§8.8) and Check the book (§8.23), and each links to the Varasto page. `stockMissing` (info) says goods were bought on 333 but no stock is recorded at the year end, which is right for a trader who sold everything. `stockTooHigh` (warning) says the counted stock is more than the opening stock plus the year's purchases. It reads only a counted list, and counts the positive entries on 333 as purchases, so the stock-change entry never inflates what was available. `stockRows` (warning) counts rows with no name, a quantity with no cost, or a lower value with no reason. In the book check `stockMissing` is silent until the year has ended, like the other after-year rules.
- **A changed list after booking.** When the booked stock-change entry no longer matches what the list now gives, Varat ja velat says so under the booking card and shows both amounts. The entry is never rewritten by itself.
- **Not in the first version:** a step-by-step checklist in the year-end dialog that walks count, value and book in order, and quantities that move with purchases and invoices. Tying the stock to the invoice product register (§8.27) is a separate, larger change.

### 8.29 Counterparties (Osapuolet)

One register of customers and suppliers, so a counterparty is typed once and every voucher with it can be found. It is the invoicing customer register of §8.27 grown a role and defaults; the code keeps the type name `Customer` and the file `customers.json` (§7.6), which invoices refer to.

- **A party** (`Customer` in `lib/domain/invoice.ts`) has the address fields of an invoice party, a `role` (`customer`, `supplier` or `both`), the invoice language and payment term, and three defaults: `incomeCat`, `expenseCat` and `vatRate` (null for the category's own). A register written before roles existed reads as customers. A VAT number of the wrong shape gets a warning in the dialog and never blocks saving; there is no VIES lookup.
- **Where it is.** "Osapuolet" is a tab of Laskut at `/app/invoices/parties`, the one place the register is listed: every party, its role and "Lisää osapuoli" in the header, which starts a party that is both. It sits under Laskut because invoices and bills to pay (§8.30) are what the parties are mostly for. The add and edit dialog is `DialogState` `customer` with an optional `role` for a new party; it asks for the language and term only of a party that is invoiced, and for the category of each side the role covers.
- **The party view** (`DialogState` `party`, `components/invoices/party-view-dialog.tsx`, `partySummary`) opens from a name in either table: the selected year's vouchers with the party, newest first, with the year's sales and purchases gross, and what is open with the party today across every year (`balancesAt` over its vouchers). A row opens the voucher. It is worked out each time and never stored.
- **An entry links to its party** with `Entry.party`, the party's id, written in the CSV's `party` column (§7.1). The description stays the user's own text, so a book read without the register still makes sense. An id no longer in the register means no link, and deleting a party leaves the id for the toast's undo, as with `reverses`.
- **The entry dialog** has "Osapuoli" under the description once the register holds anyone: a searchable picker (`VoucherPicker`) of the parties on the entry's side and the chosen one. Picking one fills the description while it is empty, the category and rate for the side (`partyDefaults`: an enabled category on that side, and the party's rate or else the category's; a rate somebody chose is kept), and on an EU sale the buyer's VAT number while it is empty. A copy keeps the party.
- **The EC Sales List** (§8.7) lists a sale under the entry's own VAT number, or else its party's (`ecSales` with the register), and shows the party names under each number.
- **The bank import** (§8.16) matches a counterparty to the register after the user's rules and before history.

### 8.30 Bills to pay (Maksettavat)

The payables side of Saatavat (§8.27): every bill received and not yet paid, with when it falls due, so nothing is paid late. A bill is an ordinary expense entry left open (§6.3); there is no separate purchase-invoice record.

- **A due date on the entry.** `Entry.due`, an ISO date, written in the CSV's `due` column (§7.1). The entry dialog's payment tab asks for "Eräpäivä" on an expense on a line that can be paid whose payment is not on its own date ("Toisena päivänä" or "Avoin"). It is kept once the bill is paid, so a bill paid late still shows when it fell due. It reports nothing and changes no figure, so like the payment date it stays editable on a locked voucher (§8.11). A copy starts without one. It is in the change log (§8.11).
- **The list** (`lib/domain/payables.ts`, `components/invoices/payables-list.tsx`, "Maksettavat" at `/app/invoices/payables`, the tab after Saatavat). Every open expense entry on a line that can be paid, across every year, as one row per voucher, the open parts of a split receipt together (§8.5) with the earliest of their due dates. Rows run by due date, the earliest first, with the undated ones last by voucher date. Each row shows the due date, how many days late it is, a pill for its group, the voucher number and date, the description (which opens the voucher) and the gross still to pay. Above it, totals for the groups: overdue, due today or within `DUE_SOON_DAYS` (7), later, and no due date, and the whole. It is worked out each time and never stored.
- **Paying** (`payBill`). "Maksa" asks for the day, today to start with, and marks every open part paid on it (`paidOnFor`, so a day equal to the voucher's own date clears `paidOn`). A filed VAT period does not stop it; a year whose Form 5 is filed does, and a change that adds to a filed return asks first, as `markEntryPaid` does. One toast undoes it, leaving alone a part changed since.
- **Not built:** a supplier's IBAN and reference on the bill, a payment file for the bank (pain.001), reminders of due dates in the calendar or the overview, and a due date filled from the supplier's payment term. The bank import (§8.16) closes a bill as it closes any open entry, by amount and text.
